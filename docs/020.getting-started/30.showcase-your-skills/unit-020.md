A few well-crafted playgrounds are a great start, but a resume has room for only so many links, and a list of playgrounds also tells only part of the story.
It shows the end results, while the hours of practice that went into them may stay invisible.

::highlight
Your public learner profile solves both problems.
::

Every iximiuz Labs account comes with a learner profile page at `labs.iximiuz.com/u/<nickname>`.
It's private by default, but once you make it public, it becomes **a single URL to put on your resume or LinkedIn profile** and share on social media.

The profile shows how much effort you've put into hands-on learning, and every number on it comes from actual practice in real Linux environments:

- **Highlights** - challenges solved, playgrounds started, and hours of hands-on practice. If you're ahead of most other learners, the profile says so, and a top 10% result earns a "Top N%" badge.
- **Learning progress** - the courses, tutorials, skill paths, and shell gyms you've completed or are working through, plus a daily activity calendar that shows how consistently you practice.
- **Learning by teaching** - up to 12 of your playgrounds, tutorials, challenges, or other materials that you choose to feature.

Pretty much every DevOps resume lists Linux, Docker, and Kubernetes among the skills.
Very few can back that list with hundreds of solved problems and months of steady practice that anyone can check with a single click.

::highlight
The best way to learn something is to try explaining it to others.
::

When you put a topic into words, the gaps in your understanding show up right away.
This works especially well in writing.
In a conversation, you can wave your hands and skip the parts you're unsure about.
A written explanation needs every step in place and in the right order, and every claim in it has to hold up.
Many of my blog posts started as an attempt to finally understand a topic, and the writing usually taught me more than the reading.

So writing about things you've just learned is already valuable for an audience of one (yourself).
It makes the knowledge stick, and you end up with notes to come back to.
On iximiuz Labs, such notes can become a [tutorial](/docs/learning-materials/tutorials) with a playground attached,
or a [challenge](/docs/learning-materials/challenges) that turns the problem you've just solved into an exercise with automated solution checks
(see [How to Publish Content](/docs/content-authoring/how-to-publish-content) to get started).

Your write-ups can stay private for as long as you like.
But once you're happy with them, publish the best ones and feature them on your profile next to your playgrounds.
The track record proves that you've put in the work, and a few well-written tutorials or challenges show that you understand the topic well enough to teach it (and can communicate clearly, too).
Together, they **make your profile stand out for hiring managers**.

Setting up the profile takes a couple of minutes:

1. Pick a nickname on the [Profile tab](/account#profile) of your account page. While there, add your name, an avatar, links to your GitHub, LinkedIn, X, or personal website, and choose a profile color.
2. Open the profile (**Your profile** in the avatar menu) and flip the **Public** toggle.
3. Pin your best playgrounds and write-ups to the **Learning by teaching** section. Only public materials can be featured.

If you'd rather not show some of the stats, you can hide the **Highlights** or **Learning progress** sections from visitors at any time.

Finally, by sharing your work publicly long enough, you'll signal your professional interests to the outside world, attract like-minded people, and **form the much-needed connections for a successful career**, gaining people's trust.
Start investing in your public profile today, one playground, blog post, or side project at a time.
